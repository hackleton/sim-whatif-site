How I make my what-if simulation videos

Every what-if reel on my page starts as one line, like “what if the Moon vanished”, and ends as a finished vertical video with voice, captions and 3D scenes.

Sim.WhatIf15 Oct 2026See the reels

What this is.

I built a custom AI workflow that I call with one command, /simulation, followed by the topic.

The AI does the heavy lifting: research, script, animation code, voice and rendering. My job is to pick the topic, check the facts and the script, and decide what goes out. This page shows the steps so you can see how it works.

Step by step.

  1. 1

    Pick the topic

    I type /simulation and the question, for example /simulation what if every mosquito vanished. That one line is the whole input.

  2. 2

    Research

    The AI searches the web for real facts, numbers and studies on the topic. Every claim in the video gets a source, so nothing is made up.

  3. 3

    Write the script

    It splits the story into 4 to 6 short stages, each with one strong fact. It writes the voiceover, the on-screen captions and a beat plan for what you see in each moment. Most reels land between 45 and 90 seconds.

  4. 4

    I check the script

    I read it, fix the facts or the tone, and approve it. Nothing is built until I say yes.

  5. 5

    Choose the opening

    The AI suggests a few ideas for the first two seconds. I pick the one that would make me stop scrolling.

  6. 6

    Build the 3D scenes

    The AI writes code that draws a low-poly 3D world for each stage, with a camera that moves, on-screen counters and captions. It reuses pieces from my earlier videos, so each new one is faster.

  7. 7

    Add voice and sound

    An AI voice reads the script. Background sound and effects are timed to each scene.

  8. 8

    Preview and fix

    I watch the video in a live preview and ask for changes in plain words, like “slow this part down” or “make the caption bigger”.

  9. 9

    Render

    The final 1080 × 1920 vertical video is exported, the volume is levelled for Instagram, and a cover image is made.

  10. 10

    Post

    The AI also drafts a caption and hashtags. I edit those and post.

Tech stack.

No video editor. No paid 3D software. Almost everything is drawn by code.

  • AI agentClaude Code, with a custom skill I wrote
  • 3DThree.js
  • AnimationGSAP
  • VideoAn HTML-to-video renderer
  • VoiceAI text-to-speech
  • ResearchWeb search, checked by me

A few videos add free public-domain images or 3D models.

Want to try it yourself?

  • Start with one small video.

    Ask an AI coding assistant to make a 20-second 3D scene in a web page, then learn to export it as video.

  • Fact-check everything.

    AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Open the sources and read them yourself.

  • Turn every fix into a rule.

    Each time I correct something, I write it down so the AI never makes that mistake again. Your own list of rules is what makes your style yours.

  • Keep a human check.

    Check before building and before posting. The AI is fast, but the taste and the final call should be yours.

  • Reuse your parts.

    Save the scenes, characters and sounds you make. Each video gets faster than the last.
